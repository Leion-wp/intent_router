# Leion Delivery Founder Sales

## Offer to sell

Sell `Leion Delivery Founding Pilot`.

Keep it narrow:

- `4-week` paid pilot
- `1 organization`
- `up to 5 repos`
- `3 workflows`: `Issue -> PR`, `PR Review -> Fix`, `Release / QA / Security Gate`
- explicit human approval on sensitive steps
- local-first execution with a lightweight control-plane story for governance, metadata, and run visibility

Do not sell:

- a generic AI coding seat
- full autonomous software delivery
- a broad Leion platform transformation
- custom consulting without a fixed pilot boundary

## Best-fit buyer

Prioritize buyers with repeated GitHub delivery work and visible process friction:

1. software agencies or delivery factories running similar issue, PR, and release motions across multiple repos
2. CTOs or engineering managers at small product teams that ship often but still rely on manual coordination
3. platform or developer productivity owners who want governed workflows rather than another prompt tool

## Trigger moments

The offer is strongest when a buyer already feels one of these pains:

- engineers repeat the same issue-to-branch-to-PR flow every week
- PR review and fix work is inconsistent, slow, or hard to delegate
- release or QA gates still depend on manual checklists and tribal knowledge
- leadership wants AI leverage but refuses opaque autonomous changes
- existing tools like Copilot help individuals but do not create a reusable governed workflow

## Qualification signals

- GitHub is central to delivery
- the team can name one repo group or workflow area to pilot first
- review, approval, or auditability matters to them
- there is one named owner who can work with you during the pilot
- they can buy a fixed `4 500 EUR` pilot or clearly route the decision to someone who can

## Fast disqualifiers

- no GitHub-centered engineering workflow
- they want a fully autonomous coding agent with no approval steps
- they only want seats, chat access, or generic productivity training
- they cannot identify one repo, one workflow owner, or one pilot window
- the pain is too vague to tie to `Issue -> PR`, `PR Review -> Fix`, or `Release / QA / Security Gate`

## Core sales narrative

Use this framing consistently:

- the pain is not lack of AI access; it is inconsistent, manual, and non-repeatable delivery work
- the product is not an autonomous coder; it is a governed workflow layer around recurring engineering operations
- the proof is not model quality in isolation; it is whether one team can run three repeatable workflows with explicit approvals
- the pilot outcome is not "AI transformation"; it is one repo group running a concrete delivery pack with a demo path and evidence

## 30-day founder targets

- `80-100` targeted outbound contacts
- `10` qualified discovery calls
- `4` live demos
- `2` pilot proposals

## Account selection rule

For each target account, write one sentence with:

- why their current delivery motion likely fits the pilot
- which of the three workflows is the sharpest entry point
- why governance or approval likely matters in their environment

If you cannot write that sentence, the account is not specific enough yet.

## Discovery goals

A strong discovery call should answer five things:

1. where the delivery workflow is currently breaking
2. which repeated workflow is the best pilot entry point
3. where approval and auditability are mandatory
4. whether there is a real owner, budget path, and pilot window
5. whether the buyer wants governed automation or actually wants full autonomy

## Discovery questions

### Workflow and pain

- Walk me through how a real issue becomes a merged PR today.
- Which parts of that flow repeat often enough that a standard workflow would matter?
- Where do PR review and follow-up fixes slow down most?
- Which release, QA, or security checks are still manual or checklist-driven?
- What breaks today when the right engineer is unavailable?

### Governance and approval

- Where would you allow AI to draft or execute steps, and where do you require explicit human approval?
- What would make an AI-assisted workflow feel too risky in your environment?
- Who needs visibility into what ran, what changed, and who approved it?

### Scope and fit

- If we piloted this, which repo or repo group should go first?
- Which of these is the best first wedge: `Issue -> PR`, `PR Review -> Fix`, or `Release / QA / Security Gate`?
- How similar are the workflows across the repos that matter?

### Buying and urgency

- Why solve this in the next quarter instead of later?
- Who would own the pilot internally if we start?
- What budget or approval path would a `4 500 EUR` founding pilot need to clear?
- What result would make you say the pilot was worth expanding?

## Demo qualification checklist

Only book a demo when at least three are true:

- there is a live GitHub-centered workflow problem
- one of the three pilot workflows maps clearly to their pain
- they accept explicit approval gates
- they can identify a pilot owner
- they are open to a paid pilot rather than a free exploration project

## Objection handling

### `We do not want autonomous code changes`

That is the point of the pilot boundary. Sensitive steps keep explicit human approval, and merge automation is optional. The offer is governed workflow automation, not blind autonomy.

### `We already use Copilot / Codex / Cursor`

Those tools help individuals. This pilot packages recurring team workflows with approval points, repeatable operating rules, and a clearer path to run visibility and proof.

### `This sounds like custom consulting`

The pilot starts from a fixed three-workflow delivery pack and fixed scope: `4 weeks`, `1 organization`, `up to 5 repos`. Adaptation is limited to the minimum needed to fit the customer workflow.

### `We need auditability`

That is part of the value proposition. The runtime stays local-first, while the control-plane story is about governance, workflow metadata, run visibility, and approval evidence.

### `We do not want cloud-hosted code execution`

You do not need it. The operating model stays local-first, which is a better fit for buyers who care about controlled execution and explicit review.

### `We already have scripts for this`

Scripts automate tasks. The pilot is about making the workflow reusable, governed, reviewable, and easier to operate across a repo group.

### `The price is high for a pilot`

The pilot is intentionally scoped to prove one repeatable delivery pack on a real repo group, not to sell vague AI experimentation. The value test is whether the team leaves with usable workflows, explicit approvals, and a repeatable demo path.

### `We are too early`

That may be true. If there is no clear repo group, no owner, or no immediate delivery pain, it is better to disqualify now and re-open when one workflow becomes urgent.

## Proof points to use carefully

Use only proof already supported by the product and proof docs:

- Leion Delivery packages three repeatable workflows: `Issue -> PR`, `PR Review -> Fix`, and `Release / QA / Security Gate`.
- Approval and review remain explicit in the flow.
- The runtime can stay local-first.
- The control-plane story is about governance, metadata, routing, and run visibility.

Do not claim yet:

- quantified time savings
- broad org-wide rollout outcomes
- full audit exports already proven in production
- fully automatic context propagation everywhere
